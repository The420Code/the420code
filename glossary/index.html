How to read an entry

The headword opens each entry: the term as the work writes it, with its symbol where it has one. Beside it is the register — which face of the work the term belongs to. Nineteen registers are in use, and each is a filter above.

Inside, the locus names the notebook, chapter or Artist’s Proof where the term is installed, and the grade is the epistemic status the work assigns it: Axiom, Theorem, Derived, Derived-Conditional, Ruled, or the single Empirical input. What is not yet derived is named as open work, never asserted. A firewall fences a symbol against a collision with another. A canon note flags a point where volumes drift or a symbol is overloaded, and states the ruling.

Two registers can live under one headword. AS, ε and the viability kernel each carry a structural reading and a formal operational one; the entry holds both, so a physicist and a philosopher reading the same word are reading about the same object.
