The first seventeen

The films made first: the axiom, the sand, the ethic, and what the physics gives back.

Seventeen films, 16:33 in all. Every film’s lines are on this page as text, in order: read it with nothing playing and you have the whole of it.

Open a row and its film loads. A closed row fetches nothing.

Two glasses, one grain

0:58▸

That is the axiom.

Imagine two glasses of water, standing side by side on a table.

Identical in every way.

Write the symmetry as one to one.

Now drop one grain of sand into one of them.

Not a handful.

Just one.

It stays where it lands.

That is the record.

The break happens now.

Written outward at α, returned at α, balanced at every instant.

Net zero.

That is the axiom.

One record exists.

Four conditions

0:55▸

Four, and only four.

For a record to exist, four things must be true.

There must be a symmetry to break —

two sides, the same. This and that.

There must be a break —

this, not that.

The break must hold —

what happened cannot unhappen.

That is the record.

The record must be constrained —

somewhere, not everywhere —

so it can be read.

Symmetry. Break. Record. Constraint.

Four, and only four.

Remove any one and no record can exist.

One record exists.

The hourglass keeps its own time

1:00▸

The flow between what could happen and what is.

Imagine an hourglass.

Now let it vibrate.

Nobody is shaking it. There is no hand on the glass, and no table under it.

The sand shakes itself.

That beat is the tick.

The sand keeps its own time.

One grain goes over the edge per tick.

That grain is the electron.

One side is dark, and it is what could happen.

The other side is light, and it is what has happened.

That is reality.

The flow between what could happen and what is.

One record exists.

One grain, six readings

1:29▸

Up and down, left and right, forward and back.

Now count what you know about one grain, from watching it fall and nothing else.

Light comes off it and reaches you.

The speed of light is the ruler, not a speed limit somebody found.

It has a body — that is mass.

Not a handful. Just one.

Something pulled it down — that is gravity.

The water pushed back.

It slowed — that is resistance.

It holds where it lies — that is grip.

And every part of that happened at a rate — that is time.

Come back tomorrow and it is lying where it fell.

Those six are the constants of the universe.

A record is written and read in the same moment.

Up and down, left and right, forward and back.

One record exists.
