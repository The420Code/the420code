Section 6

What the Observer Problem Was

The observer problem’s questions answered. Two sites, two operators, one awareness.

Several questions wearing one name. Chapter 12 took the five classical objections. This section answers the questions as posed, from §§2–5.

Claim. The observer problem’s questions are answered by §§2–5, without a new premise.

Intuition. The questions assumed a stranger outside the sand. There is none. So they dissolve rather than resolve.

Givens. §§2–5. Dissolutions Chapter 12. AP01. AP25.

Theorem — the questions answered. Each in its own terms, below.

What counts as an observer? The operator, at any site. Any configuration that couples at capacity sufficient to force non-optionality and hold the record. A plate, a counter, a rock and a person are all glass. All operators. All windows of the one interior. No privileged threshold of mind, organism, apparatus or size is introduced. The condition is physical: capacity to force and hold a record. It is the same condition for all of them. The question had no answer because it looked for a kind of thing that was not sand. There is none.

When does the outcome become definite? At the landing. At the neck, by coupling, when the grain lands and is held. Not when a narrator looks. AP01’s Actualization State measures how much irreversible record has formed. AP25’s Born rule gives the weights. The landscape gives them their shape. The glass is where the definite record is held after it has formed.

Why can the observer not be removed from the theory? A reading is a coupling. A coupling is a completed event only with a second grain. Remove the reader and you have half an event. Half an event has no reading. It is not a record. The observer could never be got out of quantum mechanics because it was never added to it. It was one of the two grains in every coupling the theory describes. Both are the operator.

Why can the observer not observe itself observing? The grain in its own aperture has not held. Only what has held can be read (§5). The pupil cannot see the pupil. This is the axiom’s +1×ε, read from the reader’s side. It is Chapter 4’s dissolution of the hard problem, with its reason supplied.

Two sites, one awareness. The friend in the laboratory and the one outside the door are two operators. Two held configurations. Two windows of the one interior. Each reads a local cross-section of the one AS from its own history (Chapter 11). What each holds, it holds under the same six constraints. The constants are universal. What each has read differs by the records it has held. The reading is relative. Once the grain has landed and held, it is the same sand for both. Holding is not relative. What was relative was which site had yet read it. Two operators. Two positions. What is aware at the two is one. The now they share is the moment each writes and reads from. Neither reads it as a record (AP52 §6). Chapters 11 and 12 applied. The hourglass adds only the picture.

Grade. Derived from §§2–5 with Chapter 12, AP01 and AP25.

Two operators, two positions, one awareness.
