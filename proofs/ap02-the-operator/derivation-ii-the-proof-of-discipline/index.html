Derivation II

The Proof of Discipline

Non-zero structure requires non-zero input; the distinction between collapse and persistence is the optimisation horizon, not character.

Why structure requires permanent cost

Derivation I showed what happens when you do nothing. This derivation shows what it costs to do something. The answer is not comfortable: structure requires permanent, non-zero input.

There is no point at which maintenance becomes unnecessary. The entropy tax is paid every day, in one of two currencies: work or loss.

Claim: In an entropic system, maintaining non-zero structure requires continuous, non-zero control. Discipline is the steady-state solution of a constrained optimisation problem.

The Operator’s Objective

The Operator minimises total action: J̄ = lim sup_{T→∞} (1/T) ∫₀ᵀ ℒ(x(t), u(t)) dt with:

ℒ(x, u) = (x − 1)² + λu²

where λ > 0 is the effort weight: a non-dimensional positive constant representing the marginal price of effort relative to structural deviation.

Its units normalise to those of ℒ, ensuring dimensional consistency across the cost function. Goal: maintain structure near x = 1 at least cost.

Steady-State Least-Cost Operating Point

At steady state (dx/dt = 0), the dynamics dx/dt = −ax + u give u = ax. Substitute into the instantaneous cost: ℒ(x) = (x − 1)² + λ(ax)²

Minimise with respect to x: dℒ/dx = 2(x − 1) + 2λa²x = 0

(1 + λa²)x = 1 x = 1 / (1 + λa²)

Then the required steady input is:

u = ax = a / (1 + λa²) > 0

Scope note: This establishes the least-cost operating point. The full time-dependent optimal control law is a separate (standard) LQR/Riccati derivation and is not claimed here.

Properties of the Operating Point

Perfect maintenance (x = 1) is feasible: set u = a. But if effort has any price (λ > 0), perfect maintenance is not the least-cost operating point.

The gap between x and 1 is what pricing reveals.

Required Control Effort

At steady state:

u = ax = a / (1 + λa²)

Properties: u > 0 always. Independent of motivation, narrative, or morality. This is the baseline discipline cost.

Stability

Unlike the greedy solution, perturbations about x decay back to the operating point. Noise is absorbed. Structure persists. The equilibrium is globally stable under the steady-state control law.

Result

Theorem II.1 (Discipline). In a system with drift rate a and effort price λ > 0, the least-action operating point is x = 1/(1 + λa²) with required effort u = a/(1 + λa²) > 0. Non-zero structure requires non-zero input.

The entropy tax is paid in one of two currencies: work (u) or loss of structure (1 − x).

Corollary II.2. The greedy strategy (Theorem I.1) minimises instantaneous cost and produces collapse. The Operator strategy (Theorem II.1) minimises cumulative action and produces persistence. The distinction is the optimisation horizon, not character.

Read that again. The difference between collapse and persistence is not willpower, talent, or virtue. It is the time horizon over which you optimise. The greedy agent and the disciplined agent face the same physics.

They choose different horizons. The physics does the rest.
