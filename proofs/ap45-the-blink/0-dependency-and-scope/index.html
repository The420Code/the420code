Dependency and Scope

Contract with the reader. Dependencies, axiom mapping, claims and refusals, the honesty box, epistemic status, switch summary, debts, items held open, relationships, symbols.

0.1 — What this paper does

AP45 prices the break. Every actualization is one singular blink, and the blink has a cost. Three things, each at its stated grade.

First. It proves, at the structural register, that the cost exists and is exactly one: one grain of distinction per event, spent on the possibility ledger as the pruning of the alternatives not taken, regardless of how many alternatives there were.

Second. It fixes the denomination. The grain is α-worth, by two arguments on two jurisdictions and by the elimination of every rival currency — and it shows the cost’s two faces: an energy face that is ordinary and unchanged, and a possibility face where the toll actually lives.

Third. It pays the flow half of D5 — the debt Notebook VII named — reconciles the three registers in which the corpus had been holding the ε, and rules D5’s closure at the wave, on the three proofs that pay it together.

The paper also computes an overlay of two channel partitions and finds one axis (§6), identifies the Born statistics as the possibility ledger’s outside face (§7), and shows two deaths with their numbers (§10).

0.3 — Axiom mapping

Axiom B is principal. The break — the single +1×ε — is what this paper prices. Its two roles, held and passing, run through every section and are never conflated: the splinter held so that anything exists, the grain spent so that this thing exists rather than that one.

Axiom S is load-bearing for Lemma 1. At exact balance the crack heals and symmetry restores; a break at balance is a non-event. The same axiom, read at channel scale, splits the balance partition of §6 ten and ten.

Axiom R is load-bearing for the ledger. The possibility ledger is the record history’s claim on what can come back; the toll is registrable only because the record keeps what is irreversible, and nothing else.

Axiom C — the constraint — is enabling. Its arena reading, c, sits among the nine channels that never close, and bounded propagation prices no crossing — which is part of why the denomination could not have been c (§5), and why the vanishing of §7 costs the constraint nothing.

0.4 — Claimed, and refused

Claimed: that every actualization is one singular blink event; that the event necessarily carries a cost; that the cost is exactly one grain, denominated α, spent on the possibility ledger as the pruning of alternatives; that the energy face of every such event is the ordinary, unchanged threshold energetics; that the Born statistics are the possibility ledger’s outside face, as an identification; that the blink is the − direction’s cost family, the structural mirror of AP44’s + direction commitment; that the axiom’s ledger can never perfectly balance, on the axiom’s own terms; and that D5’s formal account is complete across three proofs, this one paying its flow half and reconciling its registers.

Refused, in advance and in §11: any new term in any energy budget at any size; any prediction of a probability deviation; any derivation of α’s value; any repair of any fired switch; any magnitude for the per-instant shed, which is a count here and a magnitude only where a site’s own structure sets it; and any contact with any standing measured number.

0.5 — The honesty box

This proof predicts no number, and says so on every face. Its nature-facing limb already ran and died: the additional-energy reading of 16 August, shown with its numbers in §10. What survives is structure, graded.

The toll theorem stands at the structural register. Lemma 1 is canon-seated, and its fully formal statement inherits the record-algebra debt — D-BLINK.1, jointly with D-CCC.1. The denomination rests on two jurisdictions and an elimination. The Born identification is an identification, not a prediction, and the proof claims no deviation anywhere. The one-axis finding of §6 is a computed overlay, ruled at its strongest reading by the author, and fenced from arithmetic.

Of the four switches armed, none is fireable by tomorrow’s experiment on its own. KS-BLINK.2 inherits one that is — the Born rule’s, carried on two registry entries, AP25’s KS-56 and AP01’s KS-V.3 — and says so.

The interpretive note of §2 is the author’s conviction, graded and worn. The corpse of KS-NPP.1 is shown here, as it is in AP47, where a new row froze beside it. Nothing in this paper repairs it.
