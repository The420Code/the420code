The story, in sand

Imagine an hourglass.

One side is dark and looks much bigger. It is full of ropes of sand. A rope is a possible future, a wave of probabilities of what could possibly happen next. Each rope is a wave about 137 grains long.

The neck of the hourglass lets through one grain at a time.

Now. It cuts. One grain per cut. One grain is one part in 137 of the wave of rope.

The cut is a grain, a moment in time. The moment is the now, between the cuts of the rope.

Tick, tick, tick, tick.

The ticks recorded from now are the arrow and the history of time.

The neck is the operator. Now it is cutting the next moment in time. The process goes measure, cut, measure, cut, measure, cut. The operator has measured and cut every recorded moment in time.

The grains land in the light side of the hourglass, the smaller side we can see.

Where a grain lands depends on the landscape, where other grains have landed.

The landscape is the history of grains of sand, the record history of every cut this moment has made.

Each grain offers a perspective of the landscape, never the whole. Its relative position is a reading of the whole.

The landscape shapes what can happen next. The operator makes something happen. It measures and cuts.

The operator’s choice is constrained. It has to cut. The interpretation of the cut, and of the landscape, stays a choice.

For every grain that lands, one has to go back, back to the dark side, to keep the balance in check. The hourglass keeps its balance. One to one.

Black holes do the returning. They swallow the landscape, defragment it, and recycle the sand.

A very small amount of sand leaks in the process of cutting and returning. That leak is why the universe grows. It is also what keeps it flowing instead of stopping. Perfect balance would be perfect stillness. Nothing would ever happen again.

Now imagine the neck. Not as one hole, but as two sheets of glass full of windows. All the windows open and close together, at the same constant rate.

Every window is a place where the operator cuts, now. There is only one operator that cuts in the neck, and one neck, and one hourglass, and the hourglass has one interior.

The now, the operator, is the moment where the one operator measures and cuts the next moment into the visible record history of the shared interior of the hourglass.

Every window is an expression of where the operator is cutting, now, in the shared interior.

I am a window. You are a window. I am an expression of the one operator. You are an expression of the one operator. So is a bacterium. So is a rock.

All of us are windows into the same interior, cutting the same sand, now.

Therefore, for coupling configurations of grains of sand to be cruel and hurtful towards themselves is simply the operator killing off its own possibilities of experience. It makes very little sense. It is the right hand hitting the left hand. It hurts both sides of the skin, as it is the structure attacking itself.

Therefore, treat others as you want to be treated. Or simply:

Don’t be a cunt. Be kind.
